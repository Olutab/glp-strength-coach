Low-Energy Days

Playbooks for the days that do not go to plan: nausea, injection-day fatigue, poor sleep, travel, heat, illness.

12 entries. Each page states whether its numbers are SOURCED or ESTIMATED.

  • Nausea Day PlaybookCommon in the first 4–12 weeks of GLP-1 escalation; nausea reported in ~20–40% of STEP trial participants
  • Injection-Day FatigueMany users report lower energy 12–48h after the weekly dose; anecdotal pattern across GLP-1 forums and trial safety sections
  • Early Satiety DayA core GLP-1 effect; gastric emptying slows significantly, so small portions feel like large meals
  • Low-Appetite MorningAppetite is typically lowest in the morning for GLP-1 users, especially in the first 8 weeks of escalation
  • Dining Out While on GLP-1Restaurant portions are typically 2–3× what a GLP-1 user can comfortably eat; strategy is protein-first, take-half-home
  • Week 1 vs Week 12 on GLP-1Appetite, energy and tolerance shift significantly across the 12–20 week escalation schedule; training intensity should follow the dose, not the calendar
  • Constipation DayVery common on GLP-1s due to slowed gastric emptying; requires hydration and fibre.
  • Travel DaySitting for long periods increases stiffness and DVT risk; requires mobility.
  • Sleep-Deprived MorningPoor sleep increases injury risk and reduces glucose tolerance; requires caution.
  • Hot Weather DayGLP-1 users may have altered thirst cues; heat increases dehydration risk.
  • Stressful Work DayHigh mental fatigue feels like physical fatigue; requires a mental break, not a hard workout.
  • Cold or Flu DayTraining with a fever or chest infection is dangerous; requires complete rest.

Limits of this page. Training and food guidance for adults over 50 using GLP-1 medications. Not medical advice, and it does not predict results. Medication decisions belong to your prescriber. Stop and contact a clinician for chest pain, fainting, persistent vomiting, or rapid unintentional weight loss.

How this page was built. Every number is either linked to a public source (FDA labels, USDA FoodData Central, published trials, ACSM/NHS guidance) or labeled ESTIMATED with its method stated on the page. Data last checked: 2025-09-15.
