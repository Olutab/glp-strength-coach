Inverted Row

Inverted Row is a moderate-energy movement: horizontal pull under a sturdy table or with a suspension strap; 3×6–8. On a morning when the log says low, the planned swap is switch to a standing band row with lighter tension — written in advance so there is nothing to decide when you feel rough. That swap is part of the programme, not a consolation prize.

Key facts

Also known astable row, TRX row
Energy costModerate — needs a decent day
Role in the planStrength stimulus
EquipmentBodyweight
Best forreturning after a break, trained adults over 50
Low-energy swapSwitch to a standing band row with lighter tension
Joint caution (50+)Lower back; keep the pelvis tucked so the body is one straight line

Your session, step by step

Planning estimate (labeled, not measured): budget about 7 minutes per movement including 60–90 s rests, so three sets land inside a 20–30 minute session. Structure listed on this page: undefined. If the clock or your energy runs out early, stop after the last completed set — a partial session still logs.

When this is the wrong choice

Swap Inverted Row out today if lower back; the named deload exists for exactly this.

The common trap

Letting the hips sag — row the chest to the table, not the hips

Stop signals

Wrist pain during the hold; sharp low-back ache
