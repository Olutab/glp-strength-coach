Floor Chest Fly

The honest summary of Floor Chest Fly: chest isolation lying on the floor to protect shoulders; 3×10. We rate it moderate for energy, so save it for days when you feel reasonably good. The deload is named on purpose: switch to a standing band chest press. Planning the easier version before you need it is what keeps training weeks connected.

Key facts

Also known assupine fly
Energy costModerate — needs a decent day
Role in the planStrength stimulus
EquipmentLight dumbbells
Best forreturning after a break
Low-energy swapSwitch to a standing band chest press.
Joint caution (50+)Shoulders; the floor stops the elbows from dropping too low and straining the joint.

Your session, step by step

Planning estimate (labeled, not measured): budget about 7 minutes per movement including 60–90 s rests, so three sets land inside a 20–30 minute session. Structure listed on this page: undefined. If the clock or your energy runs out early, stop after the last completed set — a partial session still logs.

When this is the wrong choice

Swap Floor Chest Fly out today if shoulders; the named deload exists for exactly this.

The common trap

Lowering the elbows far below the torso; let the floor catch them.

Stop signals

Shoulder pinching or chest pain; stop immediately.
